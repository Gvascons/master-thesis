\chapter{Experimental Methodology}
\label{chap:metodologia}

This chapter specifies the experimental protocol that underpins the
dissertation's entire empirical part: 14 models from three families
(gradient boosting, deep learning, and foundation models) evaluated on
18 public OpenML datasets under a protocol of selection by internal
cross-validation and re-evaluation on external folds, with a hold-out
split off before any selection and never observed by the tuning.
Besides justifying each design decision --- optimization budgets, the
zero-shot asymmetry of the foundation models, per-family
preprocessing, and threshold-free primary metrics --- the chapter
declares the statistical-inference protocol, the policy for missing
cells, the cost-measurement protocol, the scientific-conduct contract
and, before any result, the limitations of the design itself. The
experimental design specific to the distributional distillation,
pre-registered in its own document, is presented in
Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}; here we describe only the shared
infrastructure from which it inherits data, partitions, and
hyperparameters.

\section{Overview and design principles}
\label{sec:met-visao}

The methodology was built to answer the three research questions
stated in Chapter~\ref{chap:introducao}: whether the modern families
of tabular models differ in a practically relevant way under a
uniform protocol, including when the relative advantage varies with
the problem's structure (RQ1); which multi-criteria decision
framework can guide the choice when performance ties or the leader is
operationally expensive (RQ2); and what distillation transfers in
regression, at what cost, and under which verifiable conditions
(RQ3). Four principles run through every decision in this chapter:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Strict separation between selection and measurement.}
    No data used to choose hyperparameters, architectures, or
    preprocessors takes part in the final performance estimate. This
    principle materializes in the prior hold-out --- the protocol's
    strong guarantee --- and in the declared separation between the
    selection loop and the measurement loop
    (Section~\ref{sec:met-protocolo}, which also declares the
    residual bias of the external folds).
  \item \textbf{Parity of conditions across models.} All models see
    the same partitions, the same seeds, and optimization budgets
    fixed per family (100/25 trials), with the cost asymmetry
    measured and disclosed (Section~\ref{sec:met-protocolo}) --- the
    unavoidable asymmetries (the foundation models' zero-shot
    regime) are declared and justified, not hidden.
  \item \textbf{The correct unit of analysis.} Statistical inference
    operates on datasets, never on folds --- folds of the same
    dataset are not independent, and treating them as replicates
    would artificially inflate the power of the tests
    (pseudo-replication; Section~\ref{sec:met-estatistica}).
  \item \textbf{Traceable provenance.} Every number reported in the
    dissertation originates in a versioned artifact (CSV, JSON, or
    executed notebook) in the project's public repository
    (Section~\ref{sec:met-reprodutibilidade}).
\end{itemize}

The selection obeyed four criteria, each tied to a need of the
investigation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Coverage of the three task types.} Much of the
    tabular benchmark literature restricts itself to classification;
    including regression (5 datasets, with \texttt{kin8nm} and
    others drawn from curated suites such as OpenML-CTR23
    \citep{fischer2023}) is a condition for the RQ2 decision
    framework not being an artifact of a single task type.
  \item \textbf{Wide range of sample sizes.} The
    datasets range from about a thousand (\texttt{credit\_g}) to
    581{,}000 samples (\texttt{covertype}) --- spanning two orders of
    magnitude once capped at 100{,}000. RQ1 asks, among other
    things, where the lead changes hands along the size axis;
    without wide variation on that axis, the question would be
    unanswerable by construction.
  \item \textbf{Diversity of attribute composition.} Purely numeric
    datasets, mixed ones, and one predominantly categorical extreme
    case of high cardinality
    (\texttt{amazon\_\allowbreak employee}), because the treatment
    of categoricals is one of the axes that historically separates
    the families \citep{grinsztajn2022, mcelfresh2023}.
  \item \textbf{Realistic imbalance.} Imbalance ratios from 1:1 to
    about 16:1 in classification, which conditions the choice of
    primary metrics (Section~\ref{sec:met-metricas}) and brings the
    benchmark closer to credit and risk applications.
\end{itemize}

size~$\times$~dimensionality plane, with both axes on a logarithmic

the dispersion without clumping into a single quadrant is the

Datasets with more than 100{,}000 samples (\texttt{covertype},
\texttt{year\_\allowbreak prediction},
\texttt{give\_\allowbreak me\_\allowbreak some\_\allowbreak credit})
are subsampled to 100{,}000 samples \emph{before any partitioning}
(stratified in classification; simple random sampling in regression,
the case of \texttt{year\_\allowbreak prediction}). The practice is
adopted in tabular benchmarks for computational feasibility
\citep{mcelfresh2023} and, in our case, it is also an honest scope
delimitation: with 14 models $\times$ 18 datasets under the tuning
and validation protocol described below, on a single GPU
(Section~\ref{sec:met-reprodutibilidade}), the regime studied is that
of small-to-medium tabular data --- exactly the regime in which the
dispute between families remains open. Each dataset's integrity is
recorded by a SHA-256 hash in every experiment, which allows
detecting any silent change of the data between runs.

As for data provenance, all datasets are public and obtained from
OpenML by numeric identifier and version fixed in configuration; no
personal or sensitive data is processed beyond its public form. A
labeling error discovered during the study --- the configuration key
\texttt{diamonds} pointed, from the start, to the OpenML identifier
of the \texttt{kin8nm} dataset --- was rectified with a complete
public trail, and every number in this dissertation postdates the
rectification.\footnote{The canonical record of the erratum is in
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak errata-\allowbreak diamonds-\allowbreak kin8nm.\allowbreak md};
the episode is revisited as a scientific-conduct lesson in
Section~\ref{sec:met-conduta} and among the limitations of
Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusao}.}

Three composition decisions deserve explicit justification:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Why these three families.} Gradient boosting is the
    consolidated state of practice for tabular data; tabular deep
    learning is the family with the largest volume of recent
    proposals and the greatest internal heterogeneity (from the
    regularized MLP to attention models and Kolmogorov--Arnold
    networks); and foundation models are the paradigmatic bet of the
    2025--2026 generation. Comparing families --- and not only
    models --- is what enables the variance decomposition of
    Section~\ref{sec:met-estatistica}.
  \item \textbf{Why disclose the implementations' provenance.} Five
    models (MLP, FT-Transformer, SAINT, STab, TabKAN) use our own
    implementations --- the last three reimplemented from the
    original papers for lack of a mature package; KAN uses the
    \emph{efficient-kan} backbone (MIT license) vendored with
    documented adaptations. This affects interpretation: a weak
    performance by a reimplemented model confounds the quality of
    the method with that of the implementation. We mitigate the risk
    with sanity checks against published results --- for example,
    the TabKAN reimplementation reaches a ROC-AUC of 0.914--0.915 on
    \texttt{adult}, compatible with the value of approximately 0.90
    reported by the authors \citep{tabkan2025} --- and by declaring
    every protocol deviation (for example, TabKAN is trained with
    AdamW and mini-batches, instead of the original full-batch
    L-BFGS, for uniformity with the other deep models).
  \item \textbf{Why the foundation models enter zero-shot.} TabPFN
    v2.5 and TabFM operate without any hyperparameter tuning:
    ``training'' consists of conditioning the pre-trained model on
    the training set via in-context learning
    (Chapter~\ref{chap:fundamentacao}). This asymmetry is deliberate
    --- the zero-shot regime is precisely the paradigmatic claim
    under test, and its inference parameters are fixed policies, not
    quantities tuned to our data: an 8-member inference ensemble
    (TabPFN's default, mirrored in TabFM for comparability and cost,
    although TabFM's library default is 32) and a 50{,}000-sample
    context cap per member in both models, enforced by an external
    ensemble of 4 subsamples in TabPFN and internally by the library
    in TabFM.
\end{itemize}

Both foundation models have declared architectural limits, from which
the experimental matrix's only gap follows: the \texttt{helena}
dataset (100 classes) exceeds both models' class limit. The two cells
TabPFN$\times$\texttt{helena} and TabFM$\times$\texttt{helena} are
therefore \emph{structural exclusions} --- architectural
impossibilities, not execution failures --- and the final matrix has
250 of the 252 possible cells. The statistical policy for those cells
is defined in Section~\ref{sec:met-estatistica}.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:met-protocolo}

Figure~\ref{fig:protocolo} summarizes the evaluation protocol, whose
components are justified below; in the diagram, the shaded boxes mark
the data and the stage that no selection stage observes.

\centering

\includegraphics{images/tikz-cap4-1-en.pdf}
\caption[Experimental protocol]{Experimental protocol: prior hold-out
of 20\%; hyperparameter selection by internal cross-validation (3
folds) on the pool (the remaining 80\% development split, which
supplies folds, validation, and labeling), executed once, followed
by re-evaluation on 5
external folds with the hyperparameters fixed.}
\label{fig:protocolo}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Untouched final hold-out --- why.} 20\% of each
    dataset is split off \emph{before} any cross-validation
    (stratified in classification; fixed seed 42); the remaining
    80\% (the development partition, or \emph{pool}) feeds all
    selection. Every final
    result reported comes exclusively from that set. The common
    alternative --- reporting the mean of the external folds --- is
    defensible, but the prior hold-out offers a stronger guarantee:
    even a subtle leakage error in the selection loop does not
    contaminate the final number, because no selection stage ever
    observes those data.
  \item \textbf{Why the 80:20 split.} The test fraction balances two
    finite resources, and both sides are measurable in the artifacts
    themselves. On the \emph{test} side, 20\% buys metric precision:
    on \texttt{adult} (9{,}769 test rows), the bootstrap 95\% CI of
    the hold-out AUC has a half-width of $\pm$0.005; at the grid's
    floor (\texttt{credit\_g}, 200 rows), the half-width rises to
    $\pm$0.074 --- which is why no conclusion rests on a single
    dataset and the inference aggregates datasets by ranks
    (Section~\ref{sec:met-estatistica}). Shrinking the test further
    would degrade precision with the square root of $n$; enlarging
    it would steal training data. On the \emph{training} side, the
    measured learning curves (Figure~\ref{fig:cap5-curvas}) show
    that, in the region of the cut, the primary metric is already
    nearly flat --- from the measured grid point closest to half the
    pool (40--50\% in practice) to the full pool, the median
    relative improvement is 1.2\% (maximum: 5.1\%, RealMLP on
    \texttt{jannis}) --- yet still rising precisely for the
    sample-hungry networks: ceding training data to the test would
    selectively penalize one family, which would hurt the fairness
    of the comparison. The split was fixed before any result and
    follows the field's dominant convention; being identical for the
    14 models, all gain and lose data together, which preserves the
    pairing of the rank comparisons.
  \item \textbf{Tuning and measurement: the actual design, with the
    bias declared.} Hyperparameter selection uses \emph{internal}
    cross-validation (3 folds) on the full pool, executed
    \textbf{once} per (model, dataset) pair; the 5 external folds
    re-evaluate the model with the hyperparameters already fixed,
    and the networks' early stopping uses the fold under evaluation
    itself. This design is \emph{not} classical nested
    cross-validation --- there, the search would repeat inside each
    external fold. The consequence is circumscribed, and it is worth
    stating precisely: the external-fold metrics are optimistic as
    generalization estimates, because selection observed those same
    data; here they play only internal roles (stability across
    folds, Appendix~\ref{app:tabelas}) and \textbf{no reported
    result comes from them} --- all come from the prior hold-out,
    which no selection stage ever observes.
  \item \textbf{Stratification in every classification partition.}
    Hold-out, external folds, internal folds, and the 90/10
    train/validation split of the final fit; in regression, K-Fold
    with shuffling. Under imbalance of up to 16:1, non-stratified
    partitions can leave folds almost without the minority class.
  \item \textbf{Optimization budgets 100/25 --- why the asymmetry.}
    The search uses Optuna with the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator
    (TPE) sampler and MedianPruner
    \citep{akiba2019}: 100 trials for gradient boosting and 25 for
    deep learning. The nominal asymmetry \emph{attenuates} the cost
    disparity: the design premise estimated a deep-learning trial at
    about 4$\times$ a GBDT trial, and assumed that 25 TPE trials
    would suffice for most of the landscape ---
    our own budget premise, assessed \emph{a posteriori} through the
    winning configurations and the across-seed stability of
    Appendix~\ref{app:hp-escolhidos}, not a claim from the
    literature. The realized measurements showed a larger disparity
    than the premise (the median cost per deep-learning trial was more
    than an order of magnitude above the GBDTs' --- about 57$\times$
    by the ratio of medians --- and the total search time
    1.1--140$\times$ above, depending on the model pair --- a range
    computed over per-model medians, excluding \texttt{adult}); the
    residual bias favors deep learning in search time --- which,
    even so, does not win --- and the correct reading is
    ``asymmetric budgets by design, imperfect parity measured and
    disclosed'', not equalization. One exception is declared: on
    \texttt{adult}, the 7 legacy deep-learning models were tuned
    with 100 trials, not 25 (the GBDTs use 100 throughout the study;
    TabPFN is zero-shot), and the results were kept as a
    validation anchor, so that, on that single dataset, the legacy
    architectures had 4$\times$ the budget of KAN/TabKAN; the
    dataset is excluded from the tuning-cost aggregates, and the
    direction of the bias (in favor of the legacy models, which
    still do not beat the GBDTs there) touches no conclusion. The
    GBDTs use internal early stopping (50 rounds on a validation
    set), symmetric in spirit to the deep models' stopping. The
    per-model search spaces are in
    Appendix~\ref{app:espacos-busca}, and the values actually chosen
    per dataset in Appendix~\ref{app:hp-escolhidos} --- some touch
    the edge of the space (e.g., \texttt{n\_estimators} close to the
    2{,}000 cap), a declared limitation of the space design. The
    optimization direction follows each task's primary metric
    (maximizing ROC-AUC; minimizing log-loss and RMSE;
    Section~\ref{sec:met-metricas}).
  \item \textbf{Training regime of the deep models.} At most 200
    epochs with early stopping (patience 20, monitoring the
    validation loss), batch size 256, AdamW optimizer, and
    restoration of the best state --- a single regime for the whole
    family, which prevents differences of training schedule from
    being confounded with differences of architecture. Single
    exception: RealMLP keeps the schedule of the \texttt{pytabkit}
    package (256 epochs, additive patience), because the package's
    tuned defaults are themselves the object of the model's claim.
  \item \textbf{Isolation and operational robustness.} Each
    experiment runs in an isolated subprocess; out-of-memory errors
    trigger automatic batch-size reduction and, at the limit,
    pruning of the trial --- an excessively costly configuration
    does not bring down the execution queue. The runner is
    resumable: existing results are detected and skipped, which
    makes the long runs tolerant to interruptions.
  \item \textbf{Seeds.} Global seed 42; each trial receives a
    deterministically derived seed, so that any cell of the matrix
    is reproducible in isolation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Per-family preprocessing}
\label{sec:met-preproc}

Each family receives the input in the format its literature
prescribes --- imposing a single preprocessing would artificially
penalize one of them. Every preprocessor is fitted \emph{exclusively
on each fold's training data}, never on validation or test,
eliminating that classical leakage path:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gradient boosting:} ordinal encoding of categoricals
    and median imputation; CatBoost receives the indices of the
    categorical columns and applies its native treatment
    \citep{prokhorenkova2018}.
  \item \textbf{Deep learning:} standardization of the numeric
    columns and one-hot encoding of the categoricals with a cap of
    50 categories per column. The cap is necessary to avoid
    dimensional explosion: without it, the
    \texttt{amazon\_\allowbreak employee} dataset would reach
    roughly 11{,}000 to 14{,}000 columns in the training splits
    (the sum of cardinalities of the full dataset is 15{,}626),
    exceeding GPU memory for the attention models.
  \item \textbf{Foundation models:} ordinal encoding; internal
    normalization belongs to the models themselves, by design.
\end{itemize}

Two KAN-specific adaptations are recorded as methodological
decisions: layer normalization before each KAN layer and a grid range
of $[-2, 2]$. Both follow from a concrete incompatibility: B-splines
are only defined inside the grid, and standardized inputs fall mostly
outside the original $[-1, 1]$ range proposed in \citet{kan2024}.

Four observations complete the picture. First, accuracy is
misleading under imbalance --- a trivial classifier ``gets'' 94\% on
a 16:1 dataset --- and discards all probabilistic information, which
is precisely one of the axes that distinguish the families; it is
therefore reported, but never used in conclusions. Second, the KS
(Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistic on the scores) connects the benchmark
to credit and risk practice, where it is the industry's standard
metric; and the RMSE$\times$MAE comparison exposes when the error is
dominated by tails. Third, RMSE comparisons \emph{across} datasets
are avoided for not being commensurable --- $R^2$ plays the role of
the scale-free reading.

Fourth, the \emph{threshold} metrics --- precision, recall,
specificity, balanced accuracy --- do not rank models in this study,
because they depend on a cutoff whose choice belongs to the
application and its cost asymmetry, not to the benchmark
(Section~\ref{sec:fund-metricas-clf}); yet they are not omitted
either, because it is in them that the operational cost of an error
appears. For the three protagonists of the central question
(XGBoost, TabPFN, and TabFM), the per-instance predictions of the
complementarity experiment allow opening the full confusion matrix
on the hold-out, at the standard threshold of 0.5 and with the
minority class as the class of interest --- with no cutoff
optimization, and with a sanity gate against the benchmark's
aggregate. The resulting table is in Appendix~\ref{app:tabelas}
(Table~\ref{tab:app-operacionais}), the artifact in
\texttt{secondary\_\allowbreak metrics\_\allowbreak holdout.csv}, and
the substantive reading --- what imbalance hides from accuracy and
what the choice of metric changes in the verdicts --- in
Section~\ref{sec:cap5-ato5}.

\section{Statistical-inference protocol}
\label{sec:met-estatistica}

The tests employed --- Friedman, Nemenyi, Wilcoxon, the Bayesian
analysis with ROPE --- were presented in depth in
Chapter~\ref{chap:fundamentacao}; this section declares \emph{the
application protocol}: which tests, in which order, with which
corrections, and with which sensitivity analyses. At the instance
level, the protocol adds McNemar's exact test on the discordant pairs
\citep{mcnemar1947} --- the instrument of the pre-registered
complementarity analysis that Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark} reports
(Section~\ref{sec:cap5-ato5}). The chain follows the canonical
practice for comparing multiple classifiers on multiple datasets
\citep{demsar2006, benavoli2017}, augmented with a Bayesian layer
and a variance decomposition. The whole chain was verified against
reference implementations (July 2026), which included manually
recomputing the Friedman statistic (identical to the
implementation's: $\chi^2 = 31.309$ on the binary data); in the
Bayesian layer, the original implementation returns the expected
posterior mass $E[\theta]$, not the sample fraction of the
\citet{benavoli2017} convention --- both quantities are implemented
and declared (\texttt{src/evaluation/enrichments.py}), and
Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark} reports both criteria; the record is in
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak metodologia-\allowbreak estatistica.\allowbreak md}.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unit of analysis: the dataset.} The tests operate on
    $N=10$ (binary), $N=3$ (multiclass), and $N=5$ (regression)
    datasets --- never on folds, which are not independent of one
    another (pseudo-replication). The folds feed only the
    within-dataset variance (uncertainty bands and robustness
    analysis).
  \item \textbf{Friedman omnibus test} on the per-dataset ranks,
    which assumes neither normality nor commensurability of the
    metrics across datasets. We report the classical chi-squared
    \emph{and} the Iman--Davenport correction, less conservative for
    small $N$.
  \item \textbf{Nemenyi post-hoc} with critical difference diagrams,
    $\mathrm{CD} = q_\alpha \sqrt{k(k+1)/6N}$ \citep{demsar2006};
    the diagrams' grouping logic is the canonical one: groups are
    contiguous intervals in rank order, with every internal pair
    less than the CD apart.
  \item \textbf{Pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank with Holm correction}
    (exact method for $N \le 25$ without ties), complementing the
    Nemenyi with the \emph{magnitudes} of the differences.
    \textbf{Declared limitation:} with $N=5$ regression datasets,
    the smallest attainable two-sided p-value is
    $2 \cdot (1/2^5) = 0.0625$ --- rejection at $\alpha = 0.05$ is
    mathematically impossible, and after Holm all p-values saturate
    at 1.0. Regression conclusions therefore rest on ranks and
    effect sizes, and are read as descriptive.
  \item \textbf{Effect size:} Cohen's $d$ in the paired variant
    ($d_z$), the correct form for the repeated-measures design (the
    same datasets evaluate all models).
  \item \textbf{Confidence intervals:} percentile bootstrap with
    10{,}000 resamples and a fixed seed --- consistent with the rest
    of the non-parametric framework.
  \item \textbf{Bayesian analysis with ROPE} (the signed-rank of
    \citealp{benavoli2017}): the frequentist framework does not
    license affirming equivalence (non-rejection $\neq$ equality);
    the Bayesian posterior with a region of practical equivalence
    does. The primary ROPE is $\pm 0.01$ of AUC: differences smaller
    than one percentage point are treated as practically irrelevant.
    The value is a declared convention, not a constant of the field
    --- which is why the analysis is repeated with sensitivity at
    $\{0.005, 0.01, 0.02\}$, reported in full in
    Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}.
  \item \textbf{Variance decomposition ($\eta^2$):} partition of the
    rank variance into a between-family share, a share between
    models of the same family, and a residual, via sums-of-squares
    ratios; with one observation per model$\times$dataset, the
    residual bundles the model$\times$dataset interaction and the
    noise, inseparable. One interpretation caveat is declared
    upfront: in the 11-model core (Act~I of
    Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}), the families are unbalanced --- 3
    GBDTs, 7 deep learning, and 1 foundation model --- and, with
    unbalanced families, the between-family share is mechanically
    conservative.
\end{enumerate}

The two structural exclusions (foundation models $\times$
\texttt{helena}; Section~\ref{sec:met-modelos}) demand an explicit
policy, in the spirit of open benchmarks' practice of declaring
failures instead of omitting them:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Exclusions are declared, never imputed.} Imputing
    the worst rank would punish the foundation models for a known
    architectural limitation; imputing the mean would fabricate
    data. The nonexistent cell remains nonexistent and visible.
  \item \textbf{Analyses that require a complete matrix} (Friedman,
    Nemenyi, CD diagrams in multiclass) use only the models with
    full coverage \emph{or} exclude \texttt{helena} --- the choice
    is made explicit case by case in the analysis, and the
    multiclass section is treated as descriptive ($N=3$) in any
    case.
  \item \textbf{The bookkeeping is public:} 250 of the 252 possible
    cells are filled, and the foundation models' multiclass ranks
    are averages over 2 datasets --- a fact repeated in every
    affected table.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cost axes and complementary experiments}
\label{sec:met-custo}

RQ1 and RQ2 require measuring more than predictive performance.
Three cost axes (training, search, and latency) and the
complementary analyses complete the protocol:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Training and tuning cost.} Wall-clock time recorded
    per experiment, decomposed into hyperparameter search and final
    fit, under the hardware conditions declared in
    Section~\ref{sec:met-reprodutibilidade}.
  \item \textbf{Inference latency --- protocol with declared device
    and regime.} Measured in microseconds per row (\us/row) on the
    full hold-out of the \texttt{adult} dataset: median of 5 passes
    after warm-up, with CUDA synchronization, under an identical
    batch protocol for the 14 models. Each model runs on its natural
    device --- GBDTs on CPU; deep models and foundation models on
    GPU. The resulting ratios therefore describe the
    \emph{amortized batch-serving regime} on a declared machine, not
    single-row online latency, whose differences are smaller --- the
    distinction is repeated whenever the latency axis grounds a
    conclusion.
  \item \textbf{Learning curves (RQ1).} Six representative models
    re-trained on growing slices of the development partition (from
    500 samples to the available total; 3 seeds; 5 datasets), with
    hyperparameters fixed at the values selected for the full
    dataset and the same hold-out as the benchmark --- isolating the
    effect of sample size at fixed capacity. The hyperparameter
    reuse is declared as a limitation: re-optimizing per size would
    shift absolute levels, not the direction of the crossovers.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation of the decision framework (LODO; RQ2).}
    The family recommender from meta-features is evaluated by
    leave-one-dataset-out, with a normalized regret metric against
    fixed policies --- the design prevents a descriptive tree from
    being read as a validated predictor. The protocol and results
    are in Chapter~\ref{chap:framework}.
  \item \textbf{Distributional distillation (RQ3/contribution).}
    Design pre-registered in its own document
    (\texttt{docs/\allowbreak desenho-\allowbreak experimental-\allowbreak destilacao.\allowbreak md}),
    with falsifiable hypotheses, capacity controls (the students'
    hyperparameters fixed at the benchmark's optimized values),
    out-of-fold labeling, and distributional metrics. The full
    design is presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Computational infrastructure and reproducibility}
\label{sec:met-reprodutibilidade}

All experiments run on a single machine: an NVIDIA RTX 5080 GPU
(16~GB of VRAM) and 32~GB of RAM. TabFM runs on the PyTorch backend
in bf16 precision, with the library pinned by commit at release
v1.0.1 and the weights of the \texttt{tabfm-1.0.0-pytorch}
checkpoint \citep{tabfm2026}; the hardware is declared because it
delimits the regime studied (the 100{,}000-sample cap and the
50-category one-hot cap follow, in part, from it) and because cost
measurements are only interpretable with the machine explicit.

Reproducibility rests on six mechanisms:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fixed seeds} at every level (global 42;
    deterministic derivations per trial), with bit-exact
    reproducibility where the hardware allows.
  \item \textbf{SHA-256 hash of the data} recorded per experiment,
    against silent changes of the datasets.
  \item \textbf{Versioned configurations} (\texttt{configs/*.yaml}):
    OpenML identifiers and versions, budgets, partitions, and
    metrics are configuration artifacts, not constants in the code.
  \item \textbf{Dependencies with pinned versions}, including the
    exact commit of research packages installed directly from
    repositories.
  \item \textbf{Raw results, aggregates, and executed notebooks
    versioned} in a public repository, so that every number in the
    text is traceable to an artifact.
  \item \textbf{Corrections by rectification:} errors discovered
    after the record are corrected by rectification commits that
    preserve history --- never by rewriting
    (Section~\ref{sec:met-conduta}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scientific-conduct contract}
\label{sec:met-conduta}

The work adopts an explicit conduct contract, set out in the
research program's master document before the experimental phase and
binding for all activities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No novelty claim without a dated online check.}
    Every priority claim is preceded by a recorded, dated search ---
    concrete precedent: a July 2026 check avoided claiming as novel
    a result already published by contemporary work.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration of hypotheses and decision
    criteria} before the confirmatory experiments (the distillation
    design, Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}, is the central case);
    design changes become dated addenda with reasons, never silent
    substitutions.
  \item \textbf{Negative results are reported} with the same
    prominence as positive ones --- Chapter~\ref{chap:framework}
    opens with a negative result.
  \item \textbf{Explicit distinction between smoke test, pilot, and
    result:} qualitative runs and decision pilots do not enter the
    text as evidence.
  \item \textbf{Every cited number has a traceable origin} in a
    versioned artifact (CSV, JSON, or executed notebook).
  \item \textbf{Public errata and rectification without
    rewriting.} The diamonds/kin8nm case
    (Section~\ref{sec:met-datasets}) followed this rite: canonical
    record of the erratum, a rectification commit preserving
    history, and re-derivation of all affected numbers; the episode
    is discussed among the limitations in
    Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusao}.
\end{itemize}

This contract is not ornamental: it determined concrete decisions of
scope and text throughout the dissertation, and the results chapters
reference it whenever an editorial decision (a model cut, an
exclusion, a correction) needs grounding.

\section{Limitations of the experimental design}
\label{sec:met-limitacoes}

Following the good practice of declaring the design's weaknesses
\emph{before} presenting any result, this section enumerates the
known limitations and the decisions taken in response to them.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Statistical power limited by small $N$.} With 10/3/5
    datasets per task, the tests' power is low: the combinatorial
    floor of the Wilcoxon in regression ($p \ge 0.0625$) and the
    descriptive character of multiclass ($N=3$) have already been
    declared. The mitigation is threefold: claiming no single
    winner, supporting \emph{equivalences} through the Bayesian
    analysis with ROPE, and making the limit explicit whenever it
    bites. The underlying design choice is deliberate: protocol
    depth (selection separated from measurement, untouched hold-out)
    over breadth of $N$.
  \item \textbf{One declared preprocessing idiosyncrasy.} On
    \texttt{telco\_customer\_churn}, the \texttt{TotalCharges}
    column arrives from OpenML as text and the pipeline treats it as
    a categorical with 6{,}531 levels --- under the ordinal encoding
    fitted on the training data, the vast majority of the hold-out
    receives the unknown-category code. The treatment is identical
    for the 14 models, and the effect was measured on
    the \textbf{14 models}, with both arms
    (\texttt{results/\allowbreak aggregated/\allowbreak telco\_\allowbreak totalcharges\_\allowbreak sensitivity.csv};
    13 of the 14 re-evaluated arms reproduce the recorded AUC
    exactly; STab, with stochastic inference, differs by
    $8\times10^{-4}$): the effect ranges from $-0.003$ to $+0.006$
    on the other models, but is $+0.028$ for TabFM, which jumps from
    last (0.8233) to the \textbf{best AUC on the dataset among the
    14 re-evaluated} (0.8515; second is LightGBM, 0.8505) --- and
    the dataset is exactly the worst case of the ``always-TabFM''
    policy (Chapter~\ref{chap:framework}), a reading that carries
    this caveat.
  \item \textbf{Regression networks train on the raw target ---
    effect measured.} The pipeline does not standardize the target
    for the networks in regression. A dedicated measurement (5
    networks $\times$ 5 datasets $\times$ 3 seeds, inherited
    hyperparameters --- a floor on the effect, since the search was
    done on the raw target) finds RMSE improvement in 17 of the 25
    cells, typically below 2\%, with the largest gains where the
    target's scale is large or the model is sensitive (MLP:
    $+9.6\%$ on \texttt{kin8nm}, $+5.3\%$ on
    \texttt{year\_\allowbreak prediction}; TabM: $+3.4\%$ on
    \texttt{year\_\allowbreak prediction}) and small degradations
    elsewhere (maximum $-4.3\%$). The DL regression baselines are
    therefore slightly underestimated in those cases
    (\texttt{results/\allowbreak aggregated/\allowbreak dl\_\allowbreak target\_\allowbreak standardization.csv}).
  \item \textbf{Potential contamination of the foundation models'
    pre-training.} The 18 datasets are public and widely used ---
    and foundation models are pre-trained before and outside our
    protocol, so the untouched hold-out protects against \emph{our}
    leakage, not theirs. The risk is heterogeneous between the two
    models. The TabPFN \emph{base model} is pre-trained on synthetic
    data sampled from a prior \citep{hollmann2025}; the
    \textbf{default classifier checkpoint} of v2.5 that the package
    loads without \texttt{model\_path} (our case), however, is
    \emph{Real-TabPFN-2.5}, described in the official model card as
    ``finetuned on real-data'' over 43 OpenML/Kaggle datasets --- a
    list (Appendix C.1 of \citealp{priorlabs2025}) that
    \textbf{includes \texttt{higgs}} (OpenML 23512), present in this
    benchmark. The default \emph{regressor} checkpoint is purely
    synthetic, so the entire distributional part
    (Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}) is out of this risk's reach. We
    measured the consequence by repeating the final fit with the
    synthetic-only alternative checkpoint
    (\texttt{v2.5\_default-2}) on the \textbf{twelve}
    classification datasets on which TabPFN operates in this
    benchmark (one seed). The AUC difference (synthetic $-$
    default) ranges from $-0.0051$ to $+0.0002$, with median
    $-0.0002$: on \texttt{higgs} itself --- one of the datasets
    with documented overlap; the source's list also brings a Kaggle
    ``Cardio Disease'', the probable origin of
    \texttt{cardiovascular\_disease} (a name match, not verified
    row by row) --- the synthetic checkpoint is \emph{slightly
    better} (AUC 0.8113 against 0.8111), with no sign of
    memorization, and the only two deviations above one thousandth
    are on datasets \emph{without} documented overlap
    (\texttt{amazon\_\allowbreak employee} and \texttt{credit\_g},
    $-0.005$ each)
    (\texttt{results/\allowbreak aggregated/\allowbreak tabpfn\_\allowbreak checkpoint\_\allowbreak sensitivity.csv}).
    There remains, in any case, the iterative tuning of the prior
    and the inference policies to performance on public benchmarks
    across versions --- a mild form of community overfitting to
    OpenML from which no public benchmark escapes. For TabFM, the
    technical report declares \emph{exclusively synthetic}
    pre-training --- tables sampled from structural causal models
    with randomized functional dependencies \citep{kong2026} ---
    which rules out by construction direct contamination by OpenML
    datasets (checked at the primary source on 2026-10-05) ---
    unlike, as seen, the default TabPFN classifier checkpoint. The
    weaker, unaddressable risk remains: priors designed by teams
    that know the public benchmarks. We treat this residual risk
    with three safeguards: it is declared here and revisited in the
    interpretation of the results; conclusions about foundation
    models are stated at the regime level (consistency across
    datasets), not at the individual-cell level; and the cost axis
    --- immune to contamination --- takes part in every practical
    conclusion.
  \item \textbf{Pinned, unreviewed TabFM version.} TabFM is
    evaluated at the library's v1.0.1 release, pinned by commit
    (weights checkpoint \texttt{tabfm-1.0.0-pytorch})
    \citep{tabfm2026}; its official technical report
    \citep{kong2026} appeared only in late September 2026 and has
    not undergone peer review. The results refer to that version,
    and this is declared wherever the model appears.
  \item \textbf{Double exclusion of \texttt{helena}.} The class
    limit of both foundation models makes their multiclass ranks
    averages over 2 datasets (Section~\ref{sec:met-estatistica}).
    The alternative --- removing \texttt{helena} from the benchmark
    --- would hide precisely the kind of architectural restriction
    that a study of practical decision-making should expose. To be
    precise, it is a \emph{native limit}, not an
    impossibility --- TabPFN-2.5 offers an official
    \texttt{ManyClassClassifier} that circumvents the cap by
    decomposition --- and the exclusion preserves the comparison of
    the models in their native zero-shot form, without extension
    wrappers.
  \item \textbf{Latency measured in a single regime.} The latency
    protocol (Section~\ref{sec:met-custo}) uses the amortized batch
    regime, with each model on its natural device, and
    \texttt{adult} as the reference dataset. A control measurement
    on one small and one large dataset (\texttt{credit\_g}, a
    thousand samples, and
    \texttt{cardiovascular\_\allowbreak disease}, 70{,}000; the
    benchmark's cap is 100{,}000)
    confirmed that the latency rankings among the 14 models are
    stable across datasets (Spearman rank correlation 0.78--0.95)
    and that the foundation models' context dependence has the
    declared direction (TabPFN: 840 $\mu$s/row on the smallest
    dataset, 45{,}578 on the largest). The numbers do not transfer
    to single-row online serving or to other hardware; what
    transfers --- and is what the conclusions use --- are the
    orders of magnitude between families.
  \item \textbf{Learning curves with fixed hyperparameters.}
    Reusing the full-dataset hyperparameters on all slices isolates
    the size effect at fixed capacity, but may disfavor models
    whose optimal configuration varies strongly with $n$; the
    limitation is declared next to the results.
  \item \textbf{Delimited external validity.} Eighteen OpenML
    datasets do not exhaust the space of tabular problems; the
    100{,}000-sample cap and the single 16~GB GPU delimit the
    regime studied to small and medium data. Conclusions are stated
    within these limits --- and Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}
    extends the regression validation to additional datasets of the
    OpenML-CTR23 suite \citep{fischer2023} precisely to test the
    sensitivity to this delimitation.
  \item \textbf{Construct validity.} Threshold-free primary metrics
    and an explicit multi-criteria framework reduce the risk of
    optimizing an inadequate proxy, but any scalar metric
    compresses what a real operator weighs; the framework of
    Chapter~\ref{chap:framework} treats cost and performance as
    separate axes instead of aggregating them prematurely.
\end{itemize}

With the protocol fixed --- data, models, partitions, metrics,
tests, and limitations declared --- Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}
executes it on the matrix's 250 experimental cells.

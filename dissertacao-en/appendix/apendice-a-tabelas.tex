\chapter{Complete Result Tables per Dataset}
\label{app:tabelas}

This appendix presents the benchmark's master table in disaggregated
form: the primary metric of each of the 14 models on each of the 18
datasets, on the test hold-out (20\%, seed 42), per the protocol of
Chapter~\ref{chap:metodologia}. These are
Tables~\ref{tab:app-binaria}, \ref{tab:app-multiclasse}
and~\ref{tab:app-regressao} --- one per task, because the primary
metric differs across tasks and values of different metrics are not
comparable to each other.

In each table, each row is a dataset and each column a model, in the
benchmark's canonical order (the three GBDTs, the deep models, the
KAN networks, and the two foundation models). The primary metric
follows the protocol's definition:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{binary classification} (10 datasets): ROC-AUC ---
    higher is better (Table~\ref{tab:app-binaria});
  \item \textbf{multiclass classification} (3 datasets): log-loss ---
    lower is better (Table~\ref{tab:app-multiclasse});
  \item \textbf{regression} (5 datasets): RMSE on the scale in which
    the source suite ships each dataset's target --- lower is better
    (Table~\ref{tab:app-regressao}). The scales differ across
    datasets (notes of Table~\ref{tab:datasets}:
    \texttt{california\_\allowbreak housing} has a binarized target,
    so its RMSE is the square root of the Brier score;
    \texttt{online\_news} has a log-shares target), which is why no
    RMSE aggregation across datasets is done anywhere in the study
    --- comparisons are always within a row or by rank.
\end{itemize}
The best value in each row is in \textbf{bold}. Values carry four
decimal places. The two cells marked ``---'' (\texttt{helena}
$\times$ TabPFN/TabFM) are the study's structural exclusions: the
dataset has 100 classes, above both foundation models' architectural
limit. The complete state is therefore 250 measured cells out of
$14 \times 18 = 252$ possible.

As for provenance, all numbers come from
\texttt{results/\allowbreak aggregated/\allowbreak test\_\allowbreak results.\allowbreak csv},
the benchmark's versioned aggregate; the tables themselves are
generated automatically by
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak gen\_\allowbreak appendix\_\allowbreak tables.\allowbreak py}
from that artifact (no value is transcribed by hand). The
per-fold cross-validation results feed only the variability table;
the inference of Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark} operates on the
hold-out, one value per dataset. The per-fold artifact
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak aggregated/\allowbreak fold\_\allowbreak results.\allowbreak csv},
$\sim$300~MB) is the only exception to the public-repository rule: it
stays out of remote version control for size, and is reproducible
from \texttt{results/raw} and the recorded seeds. The 15-column
tables use rotated pages, with the within-row comparison --- the main
reading --- intact in a single table per task.

The appendix is completed by the remaining instruments of
Chapter~\ref{chap:metodologia}'s statistical protocol, all generated
the same way (no value transcribed by hand): the \textbf{secondary}
metrics of each task, summarized as average rank per model
(Tables~\ref{tab:app-secundarias-binary},
\ref{tab:app-secundarias-multiclass}
and~\ref{tab:app-secundarias-regression} --- ranks because log-loss,
RMSE and MAE have incommensurable scales across datasets; per-cell
values are in the artifact), with one exception opened in full: the
\textbf{KS} statistic, the credit industry's standard metric, gets
its own table with per-dataset values (Table~\ref{tab:app-ks}) ---
KS is commensurable across binary datasets, and it is the reading
that connects the benchmark to risk practice; the \textbf{effect
size} matrices (paired Cohen's $d$,
Tables~\ref{tab:app-cohensd-binary}
and~\ref{tab:app-cohensd-regression}); the \textbf{bootstrap
confidence intervals} of the mean AUC in binary
(Table~\ref{tab:app-bootstrap-auc}; in the other tasks, $N{=}3$ and
$N{=}5$ datasets and incommensurable scales would make the
across-dataset mean uninterpretable, and the instrument is not
reported --- the same reason the study does not aggregate RMSE); the
\textbf{consolidated costs} per model (Table~\ref{tab:app-custos});
and the \textbf{across-fold variability} of the primary metric
(Table~\ref{tab:app-foldvar}), which supports the worst-case reading
of Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}.

Finally, Table~\ref{tab:app-operacionais} opens the confusion matrix
of the central question's three protagonists (XGBoost, TabPFN, and
TabFM) on the hold-out: minority-class precision and recall and
balanced accuracy, at the standard threshold of 0.5 (argmax), with no
cutoff optimization --- the operational picture that complements the
threshold-free reading of AUC, per the trade-off discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:fund-metricas-clf} and declared in
Section~\ref{sec:met-metricas}. The table is computed by
\texttt{scripts/\allowbreak secondary\_\allowbreak metrics\_\allowbreak report.py}
on the per-instance predictions of the complementarity experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:cap5-ato5}), with its own sanity gate: each cell's
recomputed accuracy reproduces the benchmark aggregate's (divergence
$\leq 10^{-3}$ in all 37 classification cells of the gate; the
table shows 36 --- the XGBoost$\times$\texttt{helena} cell passes
the gate, but the \texttt{helena} row is left out of the table
because the foundation models are excluded on it). Per-cell F1 and
specificity are in
the artifact
(\texttt{secondary\_\allowbreak metrics\_\allowbreak holdout.csv}).

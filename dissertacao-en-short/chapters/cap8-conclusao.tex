\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusao}

This chapter answers the three research questions, restates the
contributions, translates the results into advice for the
practitioner, and consolidates the limitations and future work.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:conc-finais}

\textbf{RQ1 --- does the accuracy tie survive?} Within the 11-model
core, yes, in binary classification: under a ROPE of $\pm0.01$ AUC,
8 of the 10 non-leaders are practically equivalent to the leader,
TabPFN, on the lenient criterion and 3 of 10 --- the GBDTs --- at the
strict 0.95 cutoff, a reading that holds for ROPE $\geq 0.01$; the
model$\times$dataset interaction, inseparable from noise at one
observation per cell, takes 0.69 of the rank variance against 0.20
for the family and 0.11 for the model within the family. Multiclass
and regression are descriptive. With TabFM, a consistent leader
emerges without the statistical tie being rejected: best mean rank
in all three tasks (2.7/1.0/1.2; multiclass over the two datasets
where it operates) and best model on 13 of 18 datasets --- a lead
supported by consistency and paired effect size, not by the
underpowered post-hoc nor by the Bayesian instrument, which does not
separate TabFM from XGBoost and CatBoost. It costs about five orders
of magnitude in serving latency on the reference dataset
(43{,}262 against XGBoost's 0.33~\us/row). At the instance level,
the pre-registered complementarity analysis finds median label
agreement of 0.950/0.936 with XGBoost, and none of its three
mechanistic hypotheses is confirmed.

\textbf{RQ2 --- which decision artifact holds up?} A
constraint-based policy, not a learned router. Under
leave-one-dataset-out, meta-feature routing scores below the trivial
majority rule (0.11 against 0.44 with 11 models; 0.72 against 0.78
with 14). ``Foundation model first'' has zero median regret on the
primary metric in a descriptive evaluation over the 17 datasets on
which the FMs operate; that row is an intra-family oracle, and the
executable always-TabFM policy keeps mean 0.080 and median 0.000 ---
by the mean, above the LODO tree's 0.051, a tree that nonetheless
fails as a predictor. When the decision weighs latency, the answer
changes early: in the registered analysis E2 the recommendation flips
with 10--20\% of the weight on latency, by median regret under rank
normalization (20--35\% under the alternative min--max
normalizations, the rank one flipping earliest) --- which is why the
flowchart's first question is the latency budget.

\textbf{RQ3 --- what does distillation transfer?} Part of the shape
of the predictive distribution, not the mean --- with TabPFN-2.5 as
the single teacher and in regression only. The point student beats
its control on 4 of 20 datasets (H1 refuted). The distributional
student retains 12--19\% of the teacher's CRPS advantage at the
median, a median CRPS gain of 2.8\% shrinking to 1.4\% with both arms
re-tuned, positive on 16 of 20 datasets ($p\approx0.019$ under the
sequential correction). H2 is not confirmed as a conjunction
(coverage within $\pm5$~p.p.\ on 10 of 20) and H3 is not confirmed
as registered; the student serves at 99--169~\us/row in the measured
frontier cases. The verifiable conditions condense into a decision
rule --- run the log transformation as a diagnostic, test a strong
native such as the QRF, distill only the advantage that remains, as
measured by an OOF labeling --- derived on the same 20 datasets, with
its gate verified only in the oracle version.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:conc-contribuicoes}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A 14-model, 18-dataset benchmark with Bayesian equivalence
    and cost axes under one protocol, showing the core's tie and the
    2026 shift of the top as a consistent lead, not a confirmed
    separation.
  \item An independent negative result for Kolmogorov--Arnold
    networks: bottom third on all three tasks under a uniform
    protocol, though cheap to serve.
  \item A decision framework whose learned component fails under
    LODO, whose fixed policies are compared descriptively by regret,
    and whose latency inversion point is located by E2.
  \item Distributional distillation of a tabular foundation model:
    point transfer refuted, a modest distributional transfer
    measured, and a decision rule for when to distill.
  \item Reproducible infrastructure and conduct: pinned versions,
    versioned artifacts, registered hypotheses and public errata.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{sec:conc-limitacoes}

\textbf{Internal.} The selection design is not nested: tuning runs
once per pair on the full pool, so the external-fold metrics are
optimistic and only the untouched hold-out is reported. Tuning
budgets are unequal by design (100 trials for GBDTs, 25 for deep
learning), with the realized cost disparity measured and disclosed.
On \texttt{telco\_customer\_churn} a preprocessing idiosyncrasy
penalizes only TabFM (AUC 0.8233, against 0.8515 with
\texttt{TotalCharges} numeric), and regression networks train on an
unstandardized target, which standardization improves in 17 of 25
cells. TabPFN's default classifier checkpoint is fine-tuned on real
datasets that include \texttt{higgs}; the measured sensitivity is
$-0.0051$ to $+0.0002$ AUC. The Bayesian equivalence criteria were
fixed after the analysis began and are reported with a sensitivity
sweep.

\textbf{External.} Eighteen OpenML datasets (twenty regression
datasets in the distillation), up to 100{,}000 samples, a single
16~GB GPU and pinned versions (TabFM v1.0.1, whose technical report
was not peer reviewed; TabPFN v2.5) delimit the regime; with 10/3/5
datasets per task, power is low by construction. TabICLv2 and TabPFN-3, which
predate the roster's closing, were not considered --- a limitation of
the roster --- and TabPFN-3.5, a later release, was not
evaluated.

\textbf{Construct.} E2's 10--20\% band is a property of its rank
normalization; min--max alternatives move it to 20--35\%, in the
same direction. Latency enters through one regime measured on a
reference dataset (\texttt{adult}); rankings are stable across
datasets (Spearman 0.78--0.95) and E2's crossover is identical under
the three proxies, but hardware sensitivity is unmeasured.

\textbf{Distillation.} One teacher (TabPFN-2.5, whose default
regressor checkpoint is purely synthetic), regression only, with
the spread of TabFM's ensemble predictions checked as a distribution
on a single binarized-target dataset. Two of
the four omitted CTR23 datasets overlap the study's central datasets
(17/24 with all four, $p=0.032$; 16/22 without the two, $p=0.026$). The decision rule has no out-of-sample test.
Both teachers carry non-commercial licenses.

\textbf{Multiplicity.} No correction is applied across experiments;
under Holm over the three main sign tests all stand (adjusted
$p \approx 0.019$; $0.0026$; $0.0006$), but in a family including the
supplementary instruments the headline's adjusted $p$ would be
0.057.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conc-futuros}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Repeat the distributional protocol with newer teachers
    (TabPFN-3 and 3.5 \citep{tabpfn3-2026,tabpfn35tr2026}) to test
    whether retention grows with teacher strength.
  \item Apply conformal calibration on the student beyond the
    five-dataset pilot, against the teacher-side conformal route
    \citep{park2026}.
  \item Add attention quantization \citep{kubler2026} as a fourth
    arm of the serving frontier, on the same hold-outs.
  \item Extend E2 to more criteria and to utility functions elicited
    from practitioners.
  \item Keep the benchmark living as the next generation arrives, in
    the style of TabArena \citep{tabarena2025}.
\end{itemize}

The public protocol, with versioned seeds, artifacts and errata,
exists so that re-evaluating these conclusions when models, data or
hardware change costs little to whoever comes next.

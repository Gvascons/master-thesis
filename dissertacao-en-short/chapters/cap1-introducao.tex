\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{Context and motivation}
\label{sec:intro-contexto}

``On tabular data, gradient boosting always wins.'' Few maxims of
machine-learning practice have been repeated as often over the last
decade, and this one even acquired a quantitative form. A systematic
study identified the inductive biases that explain why tree ensembles
beat neural networks on typical tables \citep{grinsztajn2022}.
Larger-scale evaluations qualified this verdict without
overturning it \citep{mcelfresh2023}. For the practitioner, the maxim
became a decision shortcut: faced with a table, use XGBoost and move
on.

Consider, however, the decision the maxim is meant to settle. A
data-science team must choose the model that will decide credit
approvals in production, a problem in the family of the
\texttt{credit\_g} and
\texttt{give\_\allowbreak me\_\allowbreak some\_\allowbreak credit}
datasets of this study. Alternatively, the team must choose the model
that will estimate
prices and durations together with prediction
intervals, as in the real-estate datasets \texttt{miami\_housing} and
\texttt{kings\_county} of the distillation study. In 2026 that team
faces options the maxim never anticipated. Besides the
consolidated gradient-boosting implementations, there are deep
architectures claiming to have caught up and Kolmogorov--Arnold
networks with strong claims of superiority. At the center of
attention are tabular foundation models, pre-trained networks that
dispense with task-specific training, because the
training set enters as context at prediction time
\citep{hollmann2025,tabfm2026}. Each proponent publishes numbers in
its own favor, measured under differing protocols and tuning budgets
and, increasingly, without peer review. The team lacks evidence
produced under a single protocol and a procedure to turn it into a
choice.

This dissertation produces both, and the results contradict the maxim
on two counts. Among the models that entered the study before its 2026 extension,
the top of the accuracy ranking in binary classification is a
statistical tie whose leader is already a foundation model. In this text, accuracy means predictive performance on each task's primary metric, and the metric of that name is called classification accuracy. The 2026 generation of foundation models, here TabFM, shifts the top in an unexpected direction: what moved
ahead was pre-training, not tuned deep learning. This lead is consistent
across datasets but
not confirmed as a statistical separation. The price changes the
nature of the decision, because the accuracy leader is about five
orders of magnitude slower to serve than XGBoost on the reference
dataset. The practical question becomes whether the leader's latency
fits the use case and, if it does not, what can be done.

That question gives the dissertation its third line of work. The
obstacle is architectural, since the training set travels with the
model on every prediction, and architectural costs can, in principle,
be transferred away. Knowledge distillation \citep{hinton2015}
trains, on the outputs of an expensive teacher, a student that serves
fast. For tabular foundation models this route was established for
classification \citep{pocketfm2026}. However, what it transfers in
\emph{regression}, when the target is the full predictive
distribution with quantiles and calibrated intervals, had no academic
answer as far as this work's dated checks of the literature reached (Section~\ref{chap:related}).

\section{Research problem}
\label{sec:intro-problema}

The problem can be stated in three layers, each building on the
previous one, and the first is a \emph{lack of comparable evidence}. The
families of tabular models available in 2026 are gradient boosting,
deep learning in its three lineages (dense networks, attention
architectures and Kolmogorov--Arnold networks) and
in-context-learning foundation models. They had not been measured under
a single protocol combining hyperparameter selection separated from
measurement, statistical inference able to
\emph{establish} equivalences rather than only reject them, and cost
axes (training time, tuning cost and serving latency) measured under the
same conditions as accuracy. The reference
benchmarks predate the 2026 generation
\citep{grinsztajn2022,mcelfresh2023}, and that generation's numbers
came mostly from its own proponents.

The second layer is \emph{decision}. Benchmarks describe, but teams
must choose. The algorithm-selection literature suggests learning a
router from meta-features of the problem \citep{rice1976}. Before one is
recommended, it must be tested out of sample in the realistic
regime of a few dozen datasets, a test the literature rarely reports. A
decision artifact remains a hypothesis until it meets
data that played no part in its construction.

The third layer is the single operational obstacle to which evidence
and decision point together, the latency of the new accuracy
leader. The problem becomes to determine what knowledge
distillation can and cannot transfer from a tabular foundation model
in regression, at what serving cost, and under which conditions
verifiable \emph{a priori}. The central focus is distributional
regression, the axis along which the teacher carries information the raw
labels do not give the student.

\section{Research questions}
\label{sec:intro-qps}

The general objective is to establish comparative evidence on the
three families of tabular models in the era of foundation models,
under a single protocol with selection separated from measurement,
and to convert it into evaluated decision instruments. This includes
how far distillation can remove the leading family's main operational
obstacle. Three research questions organize the work:

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1.] \emph{Does the accuracy tie among tabular model
    families survive a rigorous protocol (hold-out untouched by
    selection, Bayesian practical equivalence, cost axes) and the
    arrival of the 2026 generation of foundation models?}
  \item[RQ2.] \emph{Which decision artifact can a benchmark with a
    realistic $N$ of datasets actually support, a learned router or a
    constraint-based policy, and how does that answer change when
    the decision weighs accuracy against cost?}
  \item[RQ3.] \emph{What does knowledge distillation transfer from a
    tabular foundation model in regression, at what serving cost, and
    under which conditions verifiable a priori?}
\end{description}

The ``tie'' of RQ1 is the state of the literature immediately before
this study. On a good share of tabular datasets, the difference
between gradient boosting and tuned deep learning is small and the
winner alternates with the dataset \citep{mcelfresh2023}. The
``realistic'' $N$ of RQ2 is the regime of a few dozen datasets in
which any operational benchmark operates. The answers form a chain
(Figure~\ref{fig:intro-qps}). The tie and its shift (RQ1) make the
multi-criteria decision unavoidable (RQ2). The decision policy runs into
a single obstacle, latency, whose reduction is the object
of RQ3. The falsifiable hypotheses are stated where they are tested, and
E1--E3 denote the three registered complementary experiments: E1,
classical distributional baselines; E2, weighted multi-criteria
regret; E3, decision utility.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contribuicoes}

The contributions lie in measurement under a uniform protocol and in
openly reported evaluation, out of sample wherever something is learned,
and no new learning mechanism is claimed.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A multi-criteria benchmark of the foundation-model
    era}, which covers 14 models $\times$ 18 datasets (250 of the 252
    cells; two structural exclusions), with a hold-out untouched
    by selection, frequentist and Bayesian inference and three cost
    axes. In the 11-model core (the eleven models that entered the study
    before the 2026 extension), the top of binary classification is a
    statistical tie. Under a ROPE
    of $\pm$0.01 AUC, 8 of the 10 non-leaders are practically equivalent to the leader under the lenient criterion (equivalence more probable than the two alternatives combined, a much weaker claim), and 3 of 10 under the strict 0.95 cutoff, a reading that holds for ROPE
    $\geq 0.01$. The leader is already a foundation model, TabPFN
    (mean rank 3.2). With the 2026 generation, TabFM has the best
    mean rank in all three tasks (in multiclass, over the two
    datasets where it operates) and is the best model on 13 of the 18 datasets (it runs on 17 of them). This is a consistent lead that the
    underpowered tests do not confirm as a separation, at about five
    orders of magnitude more latency on the reference
    dataset (43{,}262 against 0.33~\us/row). The benchmark includes, as
    far as this work's dated checks reach, the first neutral
    TabPFN~$\times$~TabFM comparison under an identical protocol
    (Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}).
  \item \textbf{An independent evaluation of Kolmogorov--Arnold
    networks}, which is, as far as this work's dated checks reach, the first
    test of the family against the full
    GBDT/deep-learning/foundation-model grid in a multi-criteria
    benchmark. Under a uniform protocol with tuning under paired
    conditions, KAN and TabKAN finish in the bottom third on all
    three tasks, though cheap to serve. The original claims
    do not reproduce in this work's reimplementations (TabKAN reimplemented,
    KAN adapted) under this protocol (Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark}).
  \item \textbf{A decision framework that is evaluated, not merely
    described}. The learned component is evaluated under
    leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) and fails. Meta-feature routing
    scores 0.11 against 0.44 for the trivial majority rule in the
    11-model study, and 0.72 against 0.78 with 14. The fixed policies
    are compared descriptively by regret, and ``foundation model first'', credited with the better foundation model on each dataset (an intra-family oracle), has zero median regret in the 2026
    generation, a median the executable always-TabFM policy also
    attains. Experiment E2 locates the inversion of that
    recommendation at 10--20\% of the weight on latency, by median regret under rank normalization (0.1 grid), and at 20--35\% under the alternative normalizations (finer grid). The flowchart is the synthesis of
    these studies, not an evaluated policy
    (Chapter~\ref{chap:framework}).
  \item \textbf{Distributional distillation of a tabular foundation
    model}. With TabPFN-2.5 as teacher, in regression, \emph{point}
    distillation does not transfer, and H1, that the point
    student retains at least half the teacher's advantage, is
    refuted. However, distilling the quantile curves retains, at the median, 12--19\% of the teacher's CRPS advantage over a control trained on the true labels. The median CRPS gain is 2.8\%, shrinking to 1.4\% with student and control re-tuned. Retention is positive on 16 of 20 datasets
    ($p\approx0.019$ under the sequential correction). H2 adds a coverage clause to the CRPS one and is not confirmed as a conjunction, since coverage stays within $\pm5$~p.p.\ of nominal on only 10 of 20. The distilled student serves at ${\sim}10^2$~\us/row and also beats
    the best of QRF and NGBoost, both in
    default configuration, on 17 of 20 datasets (E1). Still in E1, against the best of four teacher-free alternatives chosen per dataset by an oracle, the count is 12 of 20 ($p=0.25$). Out-of-fold labeling of the teacher protects
    the student's calibration, not its accuracy. The results condense
    into a decision rule whose precondition is an \emph{advantage
    gate}: the teacher's measured advantage over the control must
    exceed the median across the 20 datasets. The gate is computed
    before the student is trained. It was verified only in its oracle version, and the rule was derived on the same
    20 datasets (Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}).
  \item \textbf{Reproducible infrastructure and conduct}: pinned versions, seeds and data, versioned artifacts to
    which every number traces, registered hypotheses and public error
    correction (Chapter~\ref{chap:metodologia}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Publications}
\label{sec:intro-producao}

The research produced two technical reports of the graduate program,
both in English, which are graded coursework without external peer
review: \emph{Does ``GBDT Always Win''? A Multi-Criteria Benchmark of 14
Tabular Models --- From a Statistical Tie to the 2026 Shift --- and
an Evaluated Decision Framework} \citep{companion2026}, condensing
Chapters~\ref{chap:benchmark} and~\ref{chap:framework}; and
\emph{Distilling Tabular Foundation Models Beyond Classification:
Distributional Regression and the Distill/Compress/Shrink Pareto
Frontier} \citep{companion-ai2-2026}, condensing
Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}. Experiment E2, the per-dataset results
of E1 and the instance-level complementarity analysis are new
relative to both.

\section{Structure of the dissertation}
\label{sec:intro-estrutura}

Chapter~\ref{chap:fundamentacao} gives the technical vocabulary
(model families, in-context learning, distillation, distributional
regression, statistical comparison, algorithm selection and regret)
and positions the work in the literature, closing with the gaps it
addresses. Chapter~\ref{chap:metodologia} specifies the protocol
shared by the empirical part and declares the design's limitations
before any result. Chapter~\ref{chap:benchmark} answers RQ1, covering
the tie of the 11-model core, the cost axes, the
conditional reversals, the shift of the top by the 2026 generation
and the instance-level complementarity.
Chapter~\ref{chap:framework} answers RQ2 with three evaluation
studies and the resulting flowchart, and Chapter~\ref{chap:destilacao}
answers RQ3 under a registered two-stage design.
Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusao} confronts each question with the
evidence and consolidates contributions, limitations and future
work. Appendix~\ref{app:suplementares} gathers the per-dataset tables
and a map to the extended technical report, which is the long version of
this dissertation (181 pages) and the destination of every
``extended report'' reference in this text. Together with the
code, artifacts and registration documents in the project
repository,\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Gvascons/master-thesis}.}
it holds the full process record, ablations and secondary analyses.
